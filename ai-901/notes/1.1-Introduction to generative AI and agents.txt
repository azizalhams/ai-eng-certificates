Large language models (LLMs) - and their more compact relations, small language models (SLMs) - encapsulate the linguistic and semantic relationships between the words and phrases in a vocabulary.
LLMs are trained to generate completions based on prompts.

Types of prompts:
System prompts that set the behavior and tone of the model, and any constraints it should adhere to.
User prompts that elicit a response to a specific question or instruction.

Retrieval augmented generation (RAG), a technique that involves retrieving information, like documents or emails, and using it to augment the prompt with relevant data. The response generated by the model is then grounded in the information that was provided.

AI agents have three key elements:
	A large language model: This is the agent's brain.
	Instructions: A system prompt that defines the agent’s role and behavior.
	Tools:
		Knowledge tools that provide access to information, like search engines or databases.
		Action tools that enable the agent to perform tasks, such as sending emails, updating calendars, or controlling devices.

1. What is a large language model (LLM)?
	A type of AI model designed to generate human-like text.
2. What is the purpose of tokenization?	
	To break down text into smaller units.
3. What are embeddings?
	Vector-based representations of tokens that capture their semantic meaning.
4. What does an attention layer do in a transformer model?
	Examines the relationships between each token and the tokens around it.
5. What is the purpose of a system prompt?
	To provide context and instructions to the AI model.
6. What is an agent in the context of AI?
	An AI system that can perform tasks on behalf of a user.
